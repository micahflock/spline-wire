# Real-world test series (plan)

[← Back to the README](../README.md) · [open-items.md](open-items.md), item 1

A plan for measuring real-world accuracy with the snap-fit chain and the website. It replaces the old test matrix (the Windows app and the O-ring dot chain). Status (2026-11-18): all five build steps done (scoring, simulation, plaque and gauge generators, the test page); ready for printing and phase 0. Nothing printed or photographed yet.

## What it has to answer

From [open-items.md](open-items.md) items 1–3, in the order the phases below take them:

1. Camera and detection: pin accuracy across phones, tilts, surfaces and lighting; whether the EXIF focal length is good enough, and what the estimate from the chain costs; lens distortion; both colourways.
2. Chain hardware: each chain's real pitch and how evenly its links come out.
3. The contact model: how close the measured curve comes to a known shape, convex, concave and through an inflection.
4. Handling: what wrapping, lifting and laying the chain flat costs, and whether a pose drifts.
5. End to end: a real object into a Fusion sketch, timed.

Each phase isolates one more source of error than the one before it. A failure is then traced to its own phase instead of showing up only in the end-to-end number.

## What has to be built first

1. **Scoring, in Python first** (the reference). *Done:* `splinewire/targets.py` (the gauges as shapes, the plaque poses), `splinewire/scoring.py`, `splinewire score PHOTO... --target NAME` or `--report REPORT.json` (with `--fit-focal` for the focal length that fits the target best), `tests/test_scoring.py`. As planned: plaque truth (pin positions after a rigid fit and after a scale fit; this exists as `--truth`); round gauges (the fitted circle's radius against the gauge's, and each contact point's distance from the fitted circle); the S gauge (contact points registered to the designed outline, then point-to-curve distances). End pins are scored with and without the ends until the custom end links exist. Then `splinewire score FOLDER` re-scores saved test photos in bulk and writes the results table. Port the scorers to TypeScript for the test page, checked with golden files as the rest of the port is.
2. **The contact model's error in simulation**, before printing anything (open item 3): render the snap-fit chain wrapped round each gauge (`synthetic.render_snap_photo`) and score it like a real photo. This gives each gauge's expected error from the model alone, so a real result can be read as model error plus everything else.

   *Done:* `experiments/gauge_simulation.py` (the chain placed as its sleeves sit on each gauge's edge; model alone over 13 placements; 6–12 photos per gauge at 0°, 20° and 35° tilt, 4032×3024, 24 mm, 230 mm away). Worst contact point without the ends, and the worst radius error:

   | Gauge | Model alone: points / spline between them | Photos, EXIF focal: worst / radius | Photos, focal estimated: worst / radius |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Convex R25 | 0.000 / 0.026 mm | 0.004 / 0.001 mm | 0.005 / 0.002 mm |
   | Convex R40 | 0.000 / 0.008 | 0.003 / 0.002 | 0.007 / 0.009 |
   | Convex R60 | 0.000 / 0.003 | 0.006 / 0.017 | 0.018 / 0.047 |
   | Convex R100 | 0.000 / 0.001 | 0.010 / 0.059 | 0.157 / **1.21** |
   | Concave R40 | 0.000 / 0.020 | 0.003 / 0.001 | 0.004 / 0.001 |
   | Concave R60 | 0.000 / 0.005 | 0.004 / 0.005 | 0.019 / 0.037 |
   | Concave R100 | 0.000 / 0.001 | 0.007 / 0.044 | **4.61 / 47.0** |
   | S | 0.017 / **0.186** | 0.009 / — | 0.009 / — |
   | Straight | 0.000 / 0.000 | 0.001 / — | 0.005 / — |

   Every photo read the whole chain. What it says:
   - The contact model is exact on arcs: each sleeve touches the arc on its pin's bisector. Its error is at the S's inflection, where the curvature reverses between two pins: 0.017 mm at the points and 0.19 mm in the spline between them. A real S that does worse than that is the chain or the photo.
   - With the EXIF focal length, clean renders measure every gauge within 0.01 mm. Real phone photos will add their own noise and distortion; this is the floor.
   - **Estimating the focal length fails on gentle curves**: the concave R100 photos come out 4.6 mm off and their radius 47 mm off. The estimate needs a strongly bent chain, which is what the existing warning says ("unreliable for nearly straight chains"). In the tests, a photo without EXIF on R60–R100 is not a measurement of the chain; the test page should show the focal source with every score.
   - The radius of a short arc is harder to pin down than its points: 0.06 mm on R100 with points within 0.01 mm.
3. **A fixed-chain plaque generator**. *Done:* `splinewire fixed-chain --pose plaque-s|plaque-tight [--links N]` (`splinewire/fixed_chain.py`, `tests/test_fixed_chain.py`). The link comes from `data/snap-link.stl.gz`, a mesh of the STEP file (`experiments/snap_link_geometry.py --mesh`, so printing needs no OpenCascade). Each joint is fused by a 3 mm cylinder that fills the pin's 0.01 mm gap to its sleeve. The result is one watertight solid, with no two links overlapping even in the tight pose, and its rims on the truth pins to 0.005 mm. It writes the whole STL, `-body`/`-rims` STLs for two-filament printers, a 50 mm scale bar, the truth and printing notes. The S pose ramps to 45° rather than 60°. Simulated (24 renders of each pose, tilts 0–40° in four directions, EXIF focal): the S reads whole every time, worst pin 0.004 mm. The tight pose reads whole in 23 of 24, cut once at 25° (and once at 30° from another direction), worst 0.010 mm: it sits at the bend limit, as meant. As planned: the real link from its STEP file (`experiments/snap_link/`), copies placed in a known pose and fused into one rigid print (pins grown into their sleeves so the joints fuse), at the real height, so the rims, the heads and the relief look as they do on the real chain. One filament change at the rim's foot (z = 12.65 mm): black then white, or white then orange. A 50 mm caliper bar on the same print checks the printer's scale. Poses: an **S** (both bend directions, joints up to about 60°) and a **tight** pose (joints at 95–102°, near the bend limit). The link count is a parameter. Truth file: the designed pin positions.
4. **A contact gauge generator**. *Done:* `splinewire test-gauges` (`splinewire/gauges.py`, `tests/test_gauges.py`): STLs, `gauges.json` and `PRINTING.txt` in `out/test-gauges/`; discs printed as rings to save plastic; the bottom 0.6 mm set in 0.4 mm against elephant's foot; no embossed labels (write the name on each). As planned: flat blocks 6 mm tall (the sleeves are widest 0.45–2.15 mm above the table, so the edge meets them there, as a low object would); the printer's edge offset is read from the discs' and the hole's diameters with calipers. A gauge covers the arc a 13-pin chain wraps on it plus a margin, so the whole chain is in contact:

   | Gauge | Shape | Joint turn | Chain covers |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Convex R25 | full disc, 50 mm | 25.6° | 307° |
   | Convex R40 | full disc, 80 mm | 16.8° | 202° |
   | Convex R60 | half disc (180°) | 11.6° | 139° |
   | Convex R100 | 110° arc segment | 7.1° | 86° |
   | Concave R40 | block with an 80 mm hole | 21.0° | 252° |
   | Concave R60 | 180° hollow | 13.4° | 161° |
   | Concave R100 | 110° hollow | 7.8° | 94° |
   | S | R60 convex into R60 concave, 90° each (188 mm of edge) | 11.6–13.4° | both |
   | Straight | a straight edge, 200 mm | 0° | — |

   Tighter gauges are left out on purpose. Below R25 a convex disc takes more than a full turn of chain, and a concave hollow below R30 needs joints near or past the ~105° limit (R15: 76°, R12: 117°). Truth file: the shape (radius and side, or the S's arcs).
5. **A test page on the site**. *Done:* `spikes/accuracy/` (its README has the steps for the owner), logic in `web/src/testbench/bench.ts`, the scorer ported as `web/src/pipeline/scoring.ts` and `targets.ts` (pinned to Python's scores to 1e-6 mm by `scoring.test.ts`), `e2e/accuracy.spec.ts`. The focal length is chosen as on the phone page: EXIF, then a lens the phone page remembers, then a number set on the page; each photo is also scored with the focal length estimated. As planned (a spike, as `spikes/decode-check/`), for one tester and one phone at a time:
   - **Setup:** the phone's name, the chain (colourway), the number of links, the target (which plaque, which gauge, or an object with no truth), and the conditions (intended tilt, surface, lighting, place in the frame). Each photo gets a test ID from these, plus a repeat number.
   - **Capture:** with the camera in the page, as the phone page takes it, or from the photo library.
   - **Measure** with the TypeScript engine three ways: the EXIF focal length, the estimate from the chain, and the saved setting if there is one. **Score** against the target's truth at once, so a bad photo can be retaken on the spot.
   - **Keep** every test on the phone with its photo. **Copy the report** (all tests as text, to paste into a message), and **Save photos** (the exact stills, EXIF intact, named by test ID) for re-scoring in Python later.

## Phase 0: calibrate (no photos)

**Results (2026-11-19, the owner's calipers; testing is iPhone only for now):**

| Check | Measured | Design | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scale bar | 49.995 mm | 50.00 | Printer scale −0.01%: fine |
| `convex-r25` outside diameter | ~49.8 | 50.00 | Outside edges ~0.1 mm in |
| `concave-r60` gap between the inner tips | ~120.2 (hard to measure) | 120.00 | Inside edges ~0.1 mm out: the same ~0.1 mm per edge shrink |
| Plaque, neighbouring rims, outside to outside | 21.5–21.6 ± 0.05 | 21.79 | The rims print ~8.55 mm across, not 8.79 (the same shrink); the plaque's pitch is exact by construction |
| Chain, neighbouring rims, outside to outside | 21.65 | 21.79 | 0.1 mm more than the plaque: a pitch ~0.1 mm long (0.8%), or rims printed larger. Not yet resolved |
| Chain straight, first rim to last | — | 164.79 | Too long for the calipers |
| Chain straight, outside of rim 1 to outside of rim 11 (10 links) | 138.35 | 138.55 at 13.00 mm | Pitch (138.35 − 8.55) / 10 = **12.98 mm** (−0.15%); 12.97 if this chain's rims are as wide as its single pair suggests (8.67), so ±0.01 mm. The single pair's 21.65 was too coarse to show it. Only one chain measured so far |

What follows:
- Read the gauges' radius errors against the printed sizes: about −0.10 mm on convex R25, +0.10 mm on concave R60, and (assumed) +0.10 mm on concave R100. The rims' centres, and so the plaques' truth, do not move.
- **The chain's pitch is 12.98 mm**, not 13.00: uncorrected, every measurement of the chain comes out 0.15% small (0.05 mm on the R25 radius, 0.15 mm on R100, 0.15 mm per 100 mm of curve). The test page now has a chain pitch setting (and the report carries it to `splinewire score`); the plaques stay at the 13.00 mm they were printed at. Measure the other chain the same way. The phone page has no pitch setting yet: phase 4 needs one, or `data/chain-snap.yaml` set to the measured pitch.
- How it was measured: a span the calipers reach, each chain straight, outside of rim 1 to outside of rim 11 (10 links), pitch = (L − 8.55) / 10, using the rim size the plaque gives; 138.55 mm for a 13.00 mm pitch, 139.55 mm for 13.10. More than ~0.15 mm off 138.55 and that chain gets a pitch setting of its own before phase 2: a uniformly long pitch only rescales the measurement, so no photo can show it.


- **Plaques and gauges:** measure each 50 mm bar. A bar off by more than 0.1 mm means the printer's scale needs fixing, or every score from that print is read after the scale fit. Check the convex discs' diameters and the concave hole's.
- **Each chain's pitch:** lay it straight and measure L across the outsides of the first and last rims (8.79 mm across each): pitch = (L − 8.79) / (links − 1). If either chain is more than 0.1% (0.013 mm) from 13.0 mm, give it a preset of its own. Pitch error scales the whole curve.
- **Rims:** look at the white and the orange rims' print quality (two perimeters) and note any gaps or blobs per link.

## Phase 1: fixed-chain plaques (camera and detection)

Truth is exact and nothing moves, so this measures the phones, the light and the software alone.

| Set | Photos | Varies |
|---|---|---|
| 1a Core | 20 | 2 phones × 2 colourways × tilt 0°, 20°, 35°, plus 2 repeats at 20° each; one plain mid-tone surface, indoor light |
| 1b Surfaces and light | 10 | 2 colourways × white paper, a dark surface, a patterned one (wood, a printed mousepad), a cluttered desk, a lamp reflected in the tops; iPhone (repeat any failure on the Android phone) |
| 1c Frame position | 2 | plaque in a corner of the frame at 20°, 2 phones (lens distortion) |
| 1d Capture route | 2 | a camera-app photo picked from the library, 2 phones (EXIF on that route) |
| 1e Bend limit | 8 | the tight pose at 0°, 20°, 35° and 40°, tilted in two directions, one colourway |

Scored per photo: whole chain found or not; worst and rms pin error after a rigid fit and after a scale fit; the fitted scale; link lengths against the pitch. Focal length three ways, plus (offline, in Python) the focal length that best fits the truth, to see how far EXIF is from it. The orange colourway's rim contrast is weaker in grey than white on black (an orange rim is roughly mid-grey against white). If 1b shows it, reading a colour channel instead of grey is a pipeline change (Python, the port and golden files).

**Proposed pass:** the whole chain found on every 1a photo; worst pin under 0.3 mm on every 1a photo with the EXIF focal length (the first plaque photo, on the old chain, gave 0.098 mm).

## Phase 2: the chain on gauges, in place (contact model and chain)

Wrap the chain round each gauge on the table and photograph it there, without lifting. One phone, 20°, both chains, wrapped twice for each gauge (straight edge once): about 34 photos.

Scored: for round gauges, the fitted radius against the gauge's and each contact point's distance from the fitted circle; for the S and the straight edge, distances after registration. Compare each with the simulated error for that gauge (build step 2): what is left over is the chain's pitch, play and print, plus phase 1's error.

**Pass** (the old targets): radius within 0.5 mm; worst contact point within 0.5 mm, end pins excluded until the end links exist.

## Phase 3: wrap, lift, lay flat (handling)

The real workflow: wrap the chain, lift it off, lay it flat somewhere else and photograph it. Convex R40, concave R60 and the S, both chains, three repeats each: 18 photos. Then creep: leave one chain posed off the R40 gauge and photograph it at 0, 1 h and 24 h.

Scored as phase 2. The difference from phase 2 is what handling costs; the spread of the three repeats is the repeatability.

**Pass:** as phase 2.

## Phase 4: end to end

Through the phone and desktop pages, not the test page:

- A pipe of known outside diameter (calipers), measured three times: radius within 0.5 mm.
- A molding profile, three times: the three curves compared with one another (no truth).
- Timed: shutter to points on the desktop page (under 15 s), and to the curve in a Fusion sketch (under 30 s).
- Fusion along the way: install the add-in with the desktop page's steps (O5), Paste points, and Insert DXF once (D19).

## Recording

- The test page's report, pasted into a message after each session, is the record. The figures go into [open-items.md](open-items.md) item 1 as a table, one row per set, with conditions. CLAUDE.md: every accuracy claim has a number behind it.
- Saved photos stay with the owner (they are large and personal), named by test ID, for re-scoring with `splinewire score` when the code changes.
- Where a real photo fails, or measures worse than its simulated twin, reproduce the condition in the simulator and add it to the benchmark.

## Effort

About 80 photos in phases 1–3, each a minute or two with the test page, and a few hours of printing: three plaques (the S in both colourways, the tight pose in one) and nine gauges in any one colour. Building steps 1–5 is a few agent sessions; step 5 depends on steps 1 and 3 for the truth it scores against.
