# The snap-fit chain

[← Back to the README](../README.md)

A chain of one printed part per link, with no hardware: the links snap together and hold a pose by the grip of a printed spring clip. Designed in Fusion by hand (the link is not generated by this repo); its settings are in [`data/chain-snap.yaml`](../data/chain-snap.yaml). The current link is **3DP_Link_Ver_MF** (2026-11-17, [`experiments/snap_link/3DP_Link_Ver_MF.step`](../experiments/snap_link/3DP_Link_Ver_MF.step)); `experiments/snap_link_geometry.py` reads every number below from its STEP file. An earlier version (13 mm pitch, 7.2 mm rim, 4.68 mm sleeves) was printed and assembled in one colour (2026-11); this one has not been printed yet.

## The link

| | |
|---|---|
| Pitch (pin axis to sleeve axis) | 13.0 mm |
| Plan size, height | 21.8 × 8.8 mm, 13.05 mm |
| Pin | hangs from a head; the same zigzag as the sleeve's bore, radius 2.0–3.0 mm, 0.01 mm inside it |
| Head | a cone over the pin, 16.7° from vertical: radius 3.15 mm at its foot, 4.395 mm at its top, 0.4 mm below the rim's top |
| Sleeve | at the tail, its top 5.05 mm below the rims' top, down to the table; outside a zigzag of radius 3.68–4.393 mm; split: a C-shaped spring clip that grips the next link's pin, under that link's head |
| Contact with the target | the sleeves (4.393 mm) and the heads' top edges (4.395 mm), both round about the pin; `half_width_mm: 4.39`. The arm between them is 5 mm wide |
| Fiducial | a rim round the head's top edge, 8.79 mm outside, 7.07 mm inside (0.80; 0.86 mm wide, two perimeters), 0.4 mm tall, printed in white |
| Joint range | +102° / −127° before the links collide (two copies of the solid turned about the pin) |

The zigzag keeps every link at one height, so all rims lie on one plane. The rim is part of the head, which is part of the pin, so it is concentric with the joint.

What changed from the first link: the rim is wider (8.79 mm, was 7.2) and as wide as the head it sits on; the sleeves are slimmer (4.39 mm, was 4.68) and sit 5 mm below the rims (was 2.4); the joint turns 4° further on its short side (102°, was 98°).

## What it changes in the software

- **Contact (`contact: round`).** The other chains touch the target with their links' straight edges; this one touches with a round sleeve and head about every pin. The curve point is the pin offset by their radius along the bisector of its two links, on convex and concave bends alike (`contact.py`). An end pin's point continues the turn of the joint next to it.
- **Detection (`detect_rims`).** The rim is too thin, and too exposed, for the ring detector. It is as wide as the head, and the next link's sleeve is hidden under the head, so straight down the camera sees the table right next to each rim wherever the arms are not; at a tilt it sees the dark near side of the head (past 16.7°), the sleeves and the table shifted against the rims. On white paper the rim then has no outer edge. Its hole (the dark head top, 0.4 mm below the rim's top) always has the bright rim all the way round, so the hole is found and measured as a dot is, dark on light, with the rim as its margin. Rings whose hole is at least 0.6 of their diameter are handled this way; wider-banded rings keep `detect_rings`.
  - The hole's outline from the threshold mask comes out a median 5–7% small (11% at most), and this rim is thin against its hole (0.24 hole radii; the first link's was 0.31). From that outline the refinement read the hole's own blurred edge as the rim and lost 4 of the 312 rims of the 24 snap-fit renders (all at 40° tilts). It now starts from the outline grown 6% (`_RIM_HOLE_GROWTH`) and finds all 312 (anything from 4% to 8% does).
  - On the renders the pins are recovered to 0.0053 mm or better on white, grey and black tables at 0–40° tilt.
- **Live camera view (`web/src/overlay/blobs.ts`).** Its quick ring test wanted the dark link just outside the rim on most rays, which the first link's sleeves gave. With this link it found no rim at all on white paper. Thin rims are now taken by their hole, as `detect_rims` takes them: round (its edge where the hole's outline is, on 80% of rays; no narrower than a 60° tilt makes it, which leaves out the dark arm between two rims), centred on the hole, with something dark just outside the rim on at least two rays (an arm), which a dark speck on a plain table does not have.
- **Bend limit (`max_bend_deg: 110`).** At a 120° bend the pins either side of the joint are exactly one pitch apart, as if joined by a link, and nothing in the photo says which order they come in. The limit covers the 102° side with perspective. Simulated, with one joint at 100–102° and tilts to 40° in 8 directions (128 photos), 110 reads the whole chain in 116 and as cut there (with a warning) in 12, all at tilts of 35–40°, never in the wrong order. 113 reads 122 whole, 5 cut and 1 with two pins bridged as one (with a warning); 116 reads 127 whole and 1 cut. Past the stop the misreads are the same whichever of the three is set. 116 would read more steep photos whole; 110 stays until real photos say otherwise. On the 127° side, a sharper bend is rarely read whole: in 72 photos of a joint at 115°, 120° or 127° (tilts to 35°), the chain read as cut at the bend in 41 and with the bend's pin left out in 29, every time with a warning that a pin is missing (whole in 2). Keep bends under about 105°, or add a stop to the link.
  - The ordering no longer steps over a pin it found as if it were missing (`order.py`): at a bend past the limit the walk could otherwise go round the bend's pin as over an undetected one, which put a pin in the wrong place without saying so. A pin that fails the size tests (half washed out by glare) is still bridged as missing. The detection benchmark is unchanged by it, photo for photo.
- **Simulation.** `synthetic.render_snap_photo` draws the chain with its relief (table 13.05 mm below the rims' top, sleeves from 5.07 mm with their zigzag, heads narrowing downwards), which `splinewire synth` uses (the snap-fit chain is the default). The realistic simulator (`scene.py`: print defects, glare, clutter) does not model this chain.

## Using it

- App: Settings → "Fill in for a chain" → Snap-fit chain. CLI: the default, or `--chain data/chain-snap.yaml`.
- Measure a printed sleeve across with calipers and put half of it in as the half width; measure the pitch over several links.
- The pin count is the number of rims (one per link).
- To check a new version of the link: `uv run --with cadquery-ocp python experiments/snap_link_geometry.py new-link.step`, then put its pitch, rim, widest radius and joint range in `data/chain-snap.yaml` and its heights in `synthetic.py` (`SNAP_*`).

## Open

- **The ends.** The first link's pin now touches with its head (4.395 mm, as the sleeves), but only at the head's top edge, 12.65 mm above the table; lower down it is the bare pin (2–3 mm). The last link's sleeve has no rim, so its position is not measured. Until there are end pieces (a loose sleeve for the first pin, a rim-only plug for the last sleeve), keep the target off both ends or discard the end points.
- **The slit.** The sleeve's slit no longer matters where the target reaches the heads: the head over every sleeve is a full circle of the same radius.
- **Real photos.** Glare on the black top surfaces and the white rim's print quality at two perimeters are untested. A matte black and a surface that is not glossy are the safe choices.
