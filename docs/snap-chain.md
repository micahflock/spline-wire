# The snap-fit chain

[← Back to the README](../README.md)

A chain of one printed part per link, with no hardware: the links snap together and hold a pose by the grip of a printed spring clip. Designed in Fusion by hand (the link is not generated by this repo); its settings are in [`data/chain-snap.yaml`](../data/chain-snap.yaml). Printed and assembled in one colour (2026-11); not yet printed with the white rim or photographed.

## The link

| | |
|---|---|
| Pitch (pin axis to sleeve axis) | 13.0 mm |
| Plan size, height | 21.3 × 9.4 mm, 12.4 mm |
| Pin | hangs from a bridge at the head; three cones 16.7° from vertical, radius 2 → 3 → 2 → 3 mm, 3.6 mm at the top |
| Sleeve | at the tail, z = 0–10 mm, the same zigzag inside and out, 1.67 mm wall, split: a C-shaped spring clip that grips the next link's pin |
| Contact with the target | the sleeves, radius 4.68 mm at their widest; the arm between them is 5 mm wide |
| Fiducial | a rim round the pin top, 7.2 mm outside, 5.48 mm inside (two perimeters), 0.4 mm tall, printed in white from z = 12 mm |
| Joint range | +98° / −127° before the links collide |

The pin and the bore are drawn 0.01 mm apart, so the clip's grip is the joint's friction, and the zigzag keeps every link at one height: all rims lie on one plane. The rim is part of the pin, so it is concentric with the joint.

## What it changes in the software

- **Contact (`contact: round`).** The other chains touch the target with their links' straight edges; this one touches with a round sleeve about every pin. The curve point is the pin offset by the sleeve radius along the bisector of its two links, on convex and concave bends alike (`contact.py`). An end pin's point continues the turn of the joint next to it.
- **Detection (`detect_rims`).** The rim is too thin, and too exposed, for the ring detector: the sleeve round each pin is 2.4 mm below the rim, so a tilted camera sees the table against the rim's far side, and the first pin has no sleeve at all. On white paper the rim then has no outer edge. Its hole (the dark pin top, on the rim's own plane) always has the bright rim all the way round, so the hole is found and measured as a dot is, dark on light. Rings whose hole is at least 0.6 of their diameter are handled this way; wider-banded rings keep `detect_rings`.
- **Bend limit (`max_bend_deg: 110`).** At a 120° bend the pins either side of the joint are exactly one pitch apart, as if joined by a link, and nothing in the photo says which order they come in. The limit covers the 98° side with perspective; a sharper bend on the 127° side makes the chain read as cut there, with a warning, rather than in the wrong order. Keep bends under about 105°, or add a stop to the link.
- **Simulation.** `synthetic.render_snap_photo` draws the chain with its relief (table 12.4 mm below the rims, sleeves 2.4 mm), which `splinewire synth --chain data/chain-snap.yaml` uses. On it the pins are recovered to under 0.01 mm on black, grey and white tables at 0–35° tilt (`tests/test_snap_chain.py`). The realistic simulator (`scene.py`: print defects, glare, clutter) does not model this chain.

## Using it

- App: Settings → "Fill in for a chain" → Snap-fit chain. CLI: `--chain data/chain-snap.yaml`.
- Measure a printed sleeve across with calipers and put half of it in as the half width; measure the pitch over several links.
- The pin count is the number of rims (one per link).

## Open

- **The ends.** The first link's pin has no sleeve round it (it touches at 3.0–3.6 mm, not 4.68 mm), and the last link's sleeve has no rim, so its position is not measured. Until there are end pieces (a loose sleeve for the first pin, a rim-only plug for the last sleeve), keep the target off both ends or discard the end points.
- **The slit.** Where the target meets a sleeve at its slit, it sits up to 0.24 mm closer; that takes a bend of more than about 40° one way.
- **Real photos.** Glare on the black top surfaces and the white rim's print quality at two perimeters are untested. A matte black and a surface that is not glossy are the safe choices.
